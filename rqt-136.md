## Exact primitive-kernel construction

Use the actual integral 22×22 Fermat quartic automorphism X and unimodular intersection Gram H from RQT-133. For each integer matrix X-I, X+I and X²+I, perform integer Euclidean column operations while tracking a unimodular V in GL(22,Z). This produces AV=[P 0] and an integral kernel basis consisting of the final columns of V. Since V has determinant ±1, those columns form the **full primitive/saturated integer kernel**, not merely a finite-index generating subset.

The resulting kernel ranks are **1, 7, 14**. Their exact Gram determinants are respectively **+4, −256, +256**. All cross-sector Gram blocks vanish. Concatenating the three 22-row integer kernel bases into Z∈M22(Z) gives det(Z)=±512 and Zᵀ H Z a block-diagonal Gram. Independently, |det(Zᵀ H Z)| = 512² · |det H| = 262144 with det H=−1.

The Smith normal form of Z has 14 invariant factors equal to 1, seven equal to 2, and one equal to 4. Therefore the finite quotient of the ambient lattice by the direct sum of its three primitive spectral sublattices is

(Z/2Z)^7 ⊕ (Z/4Z),

of order **512**. This proves that the rational orthogonal split in RQT-135 is **not** an integral direct sum of the whole lattice.

## Explicit failure boundary

This test computes primitive sector bases, their exact Gram matrices and the abstract finite quotient. It does **not yet compute explicit coset generators or an isotropic glue subgroup in the sector-discriminant forms**. That construction is the next natural bound; one must not infer explicit glue vectors from the Smith invariants alone.

The geometric marking depends on RQT-118's Pham intersection convention and RQT-121's oriented vertex intersections. This is a special Fermat K3 lattice calculation, not a proof of the general Hodge conjecture, which remains open. Three independent witnesses A/B/C, no quorum; preserve `{{ i + h }}`, `{[(i)]}`, .dlw, ENDER, cubit, full ASCII, frozen RQT-068.
