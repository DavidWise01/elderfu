## Actual geometric procedure

The Fermat quartic S is x⁴+y⁴+z⁴+w⁴=0. On w=1 let C be the positive Pham simplex patch, with coordinates x_j=ζ8 X_j and X_j≥0, Σ X_j⁴=1. Let Delta=(1−gx)(1−gy)(1−gz) C and Delta'=gx Delta. Each of the 48 Fermat lines is the intersection of equations x_a+ζ8^u x_b=0 and x_c+ζ8^v x_d=0, with u,v odd. Since each patch is a phased positive simplex, a meeting with a line can only occur at a vertex, and the w-paired coordinate determines which vertex.

Near each vertex use the remaining two complex coordinates (u,v). A local holomorphic displacement of the line is u+ζ8^e v=ε. For each of the four potentially meeting signed patch faces, solve the corresponding *real 2×2 linear system* exactly over Q(sqrt(2),i), count only strictly positive solutions, and calculate the real tangent-space 4×4 determinant to obtain the local oriented intersection sign. Include (-1)^(mask sum) from the Pham alternating sum and (-1)^j for the orientation transfer from the original simplex parameters to coordinates excluding the vertex j. The two generic perturbations ε=13/100+21i/100 and ε=−17/100+23i/100 give identical 48-entry integer pairing vectors. Each has 36 zeros, six +1 and six −1.

The raw Pham pairing is imported from the established standard vanishing-cycle intersection formula (RQT-118); the local line intersection vectors are newly computed here with exact sign tests. Since T(S) has discriminant group of exponent 8, 8 times the rational T projections of integral homology classes lie in the integral T lattice. Their Gram determinant is 64, identical to the known determinant of T(S); therefore, provided the standard Pham orientation/intersection convention applies unchanged, they form a Z-basis of T(S). This is **not** a basis of the full 22-dimensional H² lattice nor an independent analytic marking of every cycle.
