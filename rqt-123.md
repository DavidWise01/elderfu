## Geometric witness A

RQT-121 used exact algebraic elimination and an orientation expression. In RQT-123 the orientation is independently computed as the determinant of the four real tangent vectors to (a) the two oriented coordinates of the positive Fermat patch and (b) the two real directions of the displaced complex line. A separate real 2×2 linear solve determines whether a transverse intersection falls in the interior of the positive patch. Testing eight generic perturbation directions against 48 Fermat lines for each of Delta and gxDelta gives 768 numerical comparisons, **all identical** to RQT-121's integer intersection vectors. This is a separate computational derivation using the same local geometric perturbation construction; it is not independent verification of the global Pham normalization.

## Gluing witness C — new stop

RQT-114 chose a particular anti-isometry U between discriminant forms, whereas RQT-122 builds an integral Gram from actual lines and proposed Pham cycles. If an integral change of basis fixes each of the 20 algebraic line classes, then its action on the positive rank-two transcendental rational plane must preserve diag(1/8,1/8). Its integral action in the natural 8-scaled T basis must be one of eight signed permutations Q. An integral fixed-NS change of coordinates would require B^{-1}D-UQ to have integer entries. **None of the eight Q works.** This distinguishes *the particular chosen embeddings*, not the abstract unimodular lattice isometry class. A comparison map acting nontrivially on the Néron–Severi lattice may still exist.

**STOP** at constructing such a comparison map, or independently checking the full global Pham intersection and period normalization. No general Hodge conjecture proof.
